The city being founded as a true metropolis and its status as the premier political center of the country for centuries undoubtedly leads to numerous impacts of Imperial-era urban planning and spatial organization on the modern Hanoi. One of the most visible aspects lies in the historical center of the city. The Old Quarter with its 36 streets lies exactly where it was for millenia, with specific trades associated with streets creating names that endure until today (E.g, Hàng Mã, Hàng Thiếc,etc.). 

The former Imperial Citadel, now encompassed by the Ba Đình Ward, remains the seat of political authority of the Socialist Republic, with symbolic centers of power and headquarters of government being located there, including the Ba Đình Square where Ho Chi Minh declared Vietnam’s independence, the National Assembly building, the Presidential Palace, CPV Central Commit and the headquarters of the Ministry of National Defence among numerous other ministries, departments, and foreign embassies. Though French Colonialism led to the destruction of the vast majority of the Imperial Palace structures, some remain standing and are iconic landmarks such as Đoạn Môn (The Forbidden City Gate) and Chính Bắc Môn/Cửa Bắc (the North Gate). The former centerpiece of the Imperial complex, Le Dynasty-Era Kính Thiên Palace, is actively undergoing a highly accurate scientific reconstruction, along with other significant buildings along the former Imperial Axis.

As Hanoi continued its development into the future as the nation’s contemporary capital, remnants of imperial era organization and structures have been able to retain its significance and are increasingly incorporated into the fabric of a city both ancient and modern. Imperial, colonial and modern era landmarks were influenced, inspired and continued coexisting by each other, like a rich architectural tapestry recording the nation’s successive historical transformations throughout the ages. Understanding these continuities is essential to knowing the factors behind contemporary industrial, urban and infrastructural developments as well as the problems that persisted in the city’s modern incarnation.
