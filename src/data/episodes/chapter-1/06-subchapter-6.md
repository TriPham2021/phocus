Thăng Long-Hanoi throughout the ages have always been the premier political, economic and cultural heartland of Northern Vietnam, if not the entire country. Due to this unique and important position, its architecture blended cultural institutions with military fortifications, prosperous merchant districts with forbidden palace complexes and old remnants of dynasties long passed with the latest buildings serving as contemporary centers of influence. All of this was influenced by its ubiquitous geographical advantages and constraints, most of whom still controlled the city’s direction in the modern day. 

The legacy of the city’s glorious past as well as its layered urban planning still stand with us today, not just in the many ruins, temples and pagodas constructed in ages past, but also in lessons of our forefathers in urban planning as while helping us to identify how did the issues of contemporary Hanoi first stem from.
